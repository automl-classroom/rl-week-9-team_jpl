1.1 Sample Efficiency:

"ppo_dyna_ppo_comparison_sample_efficiency_rliable.png" shows the initial sample efficiency curve for 15k steps. At first sight, the curves of PPO and Dyna-PPO behave similarly.
In the beginning, Dyna-PPO might suffer from a bad underlying model, but this could also be by chance.
We thought the effect of a bad model would be much higher. On the other hand, Dyna-PPO is not able to outperform PPO significantly.

So we gave HPO with BOHB (with HyperSweeper) a try. We used the steps as fidelity and an evaluation of the negative mean return over 10 trajectories as a cost function:
"HPO2_ppo_dyna_ppo_comparison_sample_efficiency_rliable.png" and "HPO_ppo_dyna_ppo_comparison_sample_efficiency_rliable.png" show the sample efficiency.
The incumbent configs most of the time outperform the default configs significantly.
In the plot with 40k steps, we can see that the models do not improve after around 10k steps.
-> Maybe the current PPO agent is not capable of solving the environment at all.

PPO_HPO has a final return of 225 (80%: 180) after 15k steps.
-> Dyna-PPO gets to 180 at around 5000 steps.
-> PPO gets to 180 at around 8000 steps.
=> The model might boost the sample efficiency in the early training. But this effect vanishes later on.

1.2 Model Prediction Accuracy (only one seed evaluated! :() (Config before HPO was used)

"multi_step_errors_plot.png" shows the multi-step error. It intuitively shows that the state-error increases when increasing the size of the rollout.
Moreover, the model gets better overall, so the multi-step error decreases with a higher training time.
"avg_returns_plot.png" shows the development of the one-step MSE-State-error over time. After decreasing until around 10k steps, it does not improve anymore.
-> The model cannot learn from new experience. Since the agent might be able to balance the pole a bit, the observations are getting less diverse.
On the other hand, the PPO agent also does not improve after this number of steps. So maybe the agent does not deliver experience such that the model can improve.
-> This explains why Dyna-PPO's outperformance is concentrated in the early training phase, where the model is rapidly improving on diverse data.
-> Ek seems to grow exponentially. So, we have to be careful when choosing a high imag_horizon. Moreover, a high horizon might decrease the performance in the beginning of the training, since it provides poor estimates. A long horizon could also poison the training.
